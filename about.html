Scholar and researcher

Lok Pokhrel

Communication ethics, technology studies, and comparative philosophy.

Lok Pokhrel is a Visiting Research Associate in the Department of Religion at Rutgers University and a communication scholar based in Vancouver. His work sits at the intersection of communication ethics, technology studies, and comparative philosophy.

His research examines classical Indic texts, principally the Bhagavad Gita, the Yoga Sutras, and the Bhagavata Purana, as sources of moral psychology and ethical reasoning for questions of attention, judgment, and restraint under conditions of technological acceleration. He is currently developing a book manuscript on dharmic ethics frameworks for AI alignment and human judgment in technological contexts.

His research has received the International Communication Association's Best Paper Award and the Association for Education in Journalism and Mass Communication's Carol Burnett Award. He previously served as a Strategic Communications Fellow at the United Nations Division for Ocean Affairs and Law of the Sea, and has taught Media Ethics, Mass Media, Research Methodology & communication at Washington State University, Southeast Missouri State University, Minnesota State University, and Western Washington University, and served as Subject Lead in Communications at the University of Canada West.

About the Project

The project this site documents approaches classical Indic texts through philosophy, ethics, psychology, and communication. Sources are named, interpretive claims are distinguished from translations, and substantive errors are corrected publicly.

The main series reads the Bhagavata Purana chapter by chapter. A second strand examines the Bhagavad Gita, the Yoga Sutras, the Nyaya Sutras, and questions in comparative Indic philosophy as they relate to modern problems.

The project's research practices and source standards are described on the Method and Sources page. Substantive corrections are maintained in the Corrections Log.
