Research practice

Method and Sources

The series uses a defined source hierarchy. Translations, Sanskrit readings, traditional commentary, and interpretation are not treated as interchangeable.

  1. Primary translation

    Edwin F. Bryant’s Bhakti Yoga (Penguin Classics) is the primary translation where its selections cover a passage.

  2. Complete secondary source

    G. V. Tagare’s complete translation, published by Motilal Banarsidass, is used as the secondary, complete source.

  3. Direct consultation of Sanskrit

    Sanskrit is consulted directly for verses that carry the core argument. Where a Sanskrit term matters to the interpretation, the term and the interpretive choice should be made visible.

  4. A marked tradition voice

    Jayadayal Goyandka’s Tattvachintamani (Gita Press) is used sparingly as a single “tradition-voice” source. It records how the living tradition teaches something. It is not used as evidence for what the text itself says.

Translation and interpretation remain distinct. Interpretations are always marked as interpretation and kept separate from what the source translations say.

How the hierarchy is applied

The hierarchy is a practical method for keeping claims traceable. A video may draw an argument from the text, but the argument is not presented as if it were simply contained in a translation. Where sources differ, the difference should be named rather than compressed into a single authoritative wording.

This approach also supports the Corrections Log. If a reading, attribution, or factual statement is later found to be wrong, the record can identify the source of the error and the basis for the correction.
